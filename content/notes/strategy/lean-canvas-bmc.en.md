## Why a canvas

A one-page model is quick to write, show and rewrite. The point is to expose the riskiest assumptions.

## Business Model Canvas

Customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure. Good for an existing business.

## Lean Canvas

For startups, where the main risk is whether anyone needs it. Problem (and existing alternatives), segments (and early adopters), unique value proposition, solution, channels, revenue, costs, key metrics, unfair advantage. Fill problem and segments first; write the solution after the problem.

```canvas
type: lean
title: Finding replacements for imported parts
problem: Buyers spend hours matching SKF or DIN parts to local standards; a wrong match stops the line
segments: Procurement and maintenance teams at plants; early adopters are maintenance mechanics
uvp: A standard-compliant equivalent in a minute, with dimensions and load checked
solution: Search by marking; equivalence table; links to suppliers with stock
channels: SEO on part numbers; industry chats; distributor partnerships
revenue: Company subscription; commission on partner orders
costs: Development; engineers curating the database; marketing
metrics: Searches per week; share of matches found; conversion to orders
advantage: A verified equivalence database built over years
```

## Testing hypotheses

"We believe [segment] has [problem]. We'll know when [measurable result] within [time]." Test cheapest first: interviews about past behaviour, a landing page, a concierge MVP, then an MVP. Build → Measure → Learn; pivot when a hypothesis fails.

## Flashcards

```cards
Lean Canvas first blocks :: problem and customer segments
UVP :: one sentence on why you, not the alternative
Unfair advantage :: something that cannot be copied or bought
Concierge MVP :: deliver the service by hand to test demand
Pivot :: change one element of the model after a failed hypothesis
```
