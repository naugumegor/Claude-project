## Phases

- **Ferrite** (α, BCC): up to 0.02% C, soft, ductile, magnetic.
- **Austenite** (γ, FCC): up to 2.14% C, exists at high temperature, non-magnetic; what you heat to before quenching or forging.
- **Cementite** Fe₃C: 6.67% C, hard and brittle.

## Microstructures

**Pearlite**: lamellar ferrite + cementite at 0.8% C, formed from austenite at 727 °C. **Ledeburite**: eutectic at 4.3% C formed from liquid at 1147 °C.

## Key points

| Point | Composition, temperature | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| S (eutectoid) | 0.8% C, 727 °C | γ → pearlite |
| C (eutectic) | 4.3% C, 1147 °C | liquid → ledeburite |
| E | 2.14% C | steel / cast iron boundary |
| A₁ line | 727 °C | eutectoid reaction |
| A₃ line | 911 → 727 °C | ferrite starts to form |
| A_cm line | 727 → 1147 °C | secondary cementite forms |

## Classes

Hypoeutectoid steel (< 0.8% C): ferrite + pearlite; eutectoid: pearlite; hypereutectoid (0.8–2.14%): pearlite + cementite network; white cast irons above 2.14%.

Lever rule: 0.45% C steel has about $(0.45 - 0.02)/(0.8 - 0.02) \approx 55\%$ pearlite. More carbon means harder and stronger, less ductile.

## Heat treatment link

Hypoeutectoid steels are fully hardened from 30–50 °C above $A_3$, hypereutectoid from 30–50 °C above $A_1$.
